Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about SEND and EHCP processes, answered clearly and accessibly.

An EHCP (Education, Health and Care Plan) is a legally binding document for children and young people aged 0–25 who have special educational needs and/or disabilities. It describes a child's needs and the support that must be put in place to help them. An EHCP is issued by the Local Authority following an EHC needs assessment.

You can request an EHC needs assessment from your Local Authority in writing. You will need to explain why you believe your child needs an assessment and provide evidence of their needs. NLH can help you understand how to write this request effectively and what evidence to include. The Local Authority has 6 weeks from receiving your request to decide whether to carry out an assessment.

SEND stands for Special Educational Needs and Disabilities. A child has SEND if they have a learning difficulty or disability that makes it harder for them to learn than most children of the same age. This includes neurodevelopmental conditions such as autism, ADHD, dyslexia, dyspraxia, and many others. The SEND system in England sets out the support that children and families are entitled to.

An EHCP must be reviewed at least once a year through an annual review meeting. At this meeting, the school, family and relevant professionals discuss whether the EHCP still reflects your child's needs and whether the support in place is working. Following the review, the Local Authority will decide whether to keep the plan as it is, amend it or cease it. NLH can help you prepare for this meeting and ensure your views are properly represented.

If you disagree with a decision made by your Local Authority — for example, a refusal to assess your child for an EHCP, or the contents of a final EHCP — you have the right to appeal to the SEND Tribunal. Before reaching that point, there are also processes for mediation and resolving disagreements. NLH can help you understand your options and what steps to take.

Getting support from NLH is straightforward. Simply complete a parent enquiry form and tell us about your family's situation and what support you are looking for. NLH will review your enquiry and respond with information about how we can help. Guidance and support is then tailored to your child's individual needs and your family's specific circumstances.
